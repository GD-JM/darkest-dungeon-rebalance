[h1]Every Hero Worth Taking[/h1]
[i]More heroes and more skills worth taking — without changing who the heroes are.[/i]

[h2]The goal: no mandatory party[/h2]
Early on, Darkest Dungeon can become nearly unplayable without a Vestal, an Occultist or a Jester, because healing and stress relief belong to only a few heroes. This mod spreads healing and stress relief across the roster so that many different parties can work.

The same goes inside each hero: skills and ranks are rebalanced so you are no longer locked into one optimal set of four skills, repeated from one rank.

[h2]One integrated patch, same overall difficulty[/h2]
Most hero mods change one hero at a time, and that usually means making that hero stronger. This mod rebalances all 20 heroes together under one set of rules: every buff is paired with a cost or a nerf elsewhere, so the game gets neither easier nor harder overall. By our hero-strength model, total strength across the roster stays within 1% of vanilla, while the gap between the strongest and weakest heroes narrows: the Duelist and Houndmaster come down, the Bounty Hunter and Arbalest come up.

[h2]How the changes were decided[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Healing and stress relief aren't owned by a few heroes.[/b]
Example: Hellion's Adrenaline Rush (heal 3–7) and Grave Robber's Toxin Trickery (1–5) became real self-heals, and Man-at-Arms' Bolster became a party stress heal usable every turn.
[*][b]No "correct" four skills, no "correct" rank.[/b] Skills and ranks are tuned so many combinations actually work instead of one locked build.
Example: the Highwayman's riposte moved from Duelist's Advance to Tracking Shot, so a ranged Highwayman can counterattack without stepping forward.
[*][b]Follow the base game's own rules.[/b]
Example: base-game heroes gain only 2 speed over all weapon upgrades (Arbalest 3→5); Shieldbreaker (5→9) and Flagellant (6→9) were the outliers. Both now gain 2.
[*][b]Every buff has a price.[/b] What goes up somewhere comes down elsewhere, so overall difficulty neither inflates nor deflates.
Example: Vestal's Judgement went from −25% to 0% damage, and Houndmaster's Hound's Rush from 0% to −50% (its bleed went up instead).
[*][b]A skill should do what it looks like.[/b] Art and function match at a glance.
Example: a bandage stops bleeding (Arbalest's Battlefield Bandage), and the Hellion, who never tires, no longer gets exhausted.
[*][b]The lore sets the direction.[/b]
Example: the Leper was once a king, so Intimidate became Sunder, a strike that leads the fight by cracking armor instead of a support debuff. The Antiquarian earns gold rather than fights, so she stays weak, and nothing in the Houndmaster's lore justifies extra bleed and blight resist.
[*][b]Trinkets stay fixed; the patch fits around them.[/b] This keeps them compatible.
Example: the Musketeer's trinkets boost debuff chance, so her new Smokescreen still carries a small accuracy debuff.
[/list]

[h2]Showcase: the Musketeer[/h2]
The original Musketeer was an exact copy of the Arbalest, so she was rebuilt from her official lore: a celebrated sharpshooter who wants to become a legend by hunting monsters after a disappointing tournament, and who keeps her distance for lack of real combat experience.
[list]
[*][b]Piercing Ball[/b] (ACC 95): reaches every rank and pierces 10–30% armor; her bonus vs marked targets is far smaller than the Arbalest's.
[*][b]Smokescreen[/b] (3 per battle): stuns enemy ranks 1–2; stunned enemies vanish in the smoke, so the party strikes the back line.
[*][b]Covering Smoke[/b]: hides one ally for 2 turns so enemies can't single them out — protection without anyone taking the hits.
[*][b]Mark the Quarry[/b]: "This prize is mine!" She marks her prey, strips 10–20% of its armor and gains speed for 3 turns.
[*][b]Flushing Shot, Sidearm, Patch Up[/b]: drives ranks 2–3 back, shoots and steps back when pushed forward, and adds a little stress relief to healing.
[/list]
The price: lower HP (24 vs the Arbalest's 27) and speed (2 vs 3). She can change ranks, so she is tuned slightly below the rank-locked Arbalest.

[h2]Hero by hero[/h2]
The images above show every changed skill, before and after.
[b]Abomination[/b] — transforming is a real choice: Transform now relieves his own stress, and Slam works from rank 4.
[b]Antiquarian[/b] — still weaker by design (she earns gold), but Flashpowder now burns and sets up burn combos.
[b]Arbalest[/b] — a field medic too: Battlefield Bandage cures bleed, Rallying Flare reliably relieves party stress.
[b]Bounty Hunter[/b] — mark, hook, execute: Mark for Death makes the target take more damage, Come Hither pulls 3 ranks into Finish Him's reach; Uppercut is a rank-1 execution stun.
[b]Crusader[/b] — a support healer: Battle Heal boosts healing received; Inspiring Cry grows every level.
[b]Duelist[/b] — one of the strongest heroes, trimmed: Touché no longer makes her almost unhittable.
[b]Flagellant[/b] — back to the base game's rules: resists and speed growth normalized; Endure relieves stress from level 1.
[b]Grave Robber[/b] — Toxin Trickery becomes a repeatable self-heal; Thrown Dagger hits marked targets harder.
[b]Hellion[/b] — exhaustion removed: Barbaric YAWP! fires her up and weakens every enemy; Adrenaline Rush is her self-heal.
[b]Highwayman[/b] — ranged and melee builds both work: Tracking Shot grants riposte without moving.
[b]Houndmaster[/b] — a ranged bleed specialist: his hounds bite any rank and make it bleed; Target Whistle makes the prey take more crits.
[b]Jester[/b] — still the best stress healer, but Inspiring Tune works from rank 2 and Solo from any rank.
[b]Leper[/b] — Intimidate is now Sunder, an armor-cracking strike, and Revenge's cost now grows with level, so it is usable early.
[b]Man-at-Arms[/b] — the veteran who holds the line: Bolster is a repeatable party stress heal; Command adds protection.
[b]Occultist[/b] — Vulnerability Hex lowers stun, bleed, blight and debuff resist, setting up the whole party.
[b]Plague Doctor[/b] — Battlefield Medicine is the blight-cure heal (3–7) while the Arbalest covers bleed.
[b]Runaway[/b] — Ransack no longer reaches rank 4.
[b]Musketeer[/b] — see the showcase above.
[b]Shieldbreaker[/b] — the DLC outlier, back in line: Pierce loses rank 4, weapon growth standardized.
[b]Vestal[/b] — can work from rank 2: Mace Bash heals herself, Divine Grace and Illumination work from any rank.

[h2]Compatibility[/h2]
[b]Requires the DLC heroes it changes:[/b] The Crimson Court (Flagellant), The Shieldbreaker, Duelist, Runaway and The Musketeer (free). Without them the game may fail to load.
Replaces only the hero files above (plus effect, buff and skill-name files); conflicts with mods that replace the same heroes. Safe on existing saves.
With [i]UI Enhancement[/i], the Musketeer's skills show their original names (they still work as described).

Feedback is welcome — every change has a written reason, and I'm happy to explain or adjust.
